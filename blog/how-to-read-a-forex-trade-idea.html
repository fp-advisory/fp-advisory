How to Read a Forex Trade Idea: Entry, Stop Loss and Take Profit Explained

16 September 2026 · 2 min read

If you've ever looked at a trade idea and seen "Entry 1.27400, Stop 1.26800, Target 1.28700" and had no idea what to do with that, you're not alone. These three numbers are the entire structure of a trade — everything else is commentary.

Entry: where you get in

The entry price is simply the level at which the idea suggests opening the trade. It's not necessarily the current market price — a lot of ideas are built around price reaching a specific level first (a support zone, a breakout point, a retest of resistance) rather than jumping in wherever the market happens to be right now.

If the entry hasn't been reached yet, the idea is still valid — it just hasn't triggered.

Stop loss: where you're wrong

The stop loss is the price at which the idea is invalidated. If the market reaches it, the trade closes automatically for a defined, known loss — not a guess, not "I'll get out when it feels bad."

This is the number that actually matters most, and it's the one people skip past fastest. The stop isn't a formality — it's what turns "I have a feeling about GBPUSD" into an actual risk you can size and live with. A trade idea without a clearly reasoned stop level isn't a trade idea, it's a hope.

Take profit: where you're right

The take profit (sometimes shown as "Target") is the price at which the trade closes for a gain. Like the stop, it's set in advance — decided by the setup itself (a resistance level, a prior swing high, a measured move), not adjusted on the fly based on how the trade "feels" once it's open.

Why the ratio between them matters

Put the three together and you get the risk/reward ratio, usually written as something like "1.8 : 1" or "2.2 : 1". It's the distance from entry to target, divided by the distance from entry to stop.

A 2:1 ratio means the target is twice as far away as the stop — so if the idea plays out even slightly less than half the time, the maths can still work in your favour over a run of trades. This is the actual reason professional trading talks so much about risk/reward: it's not about being right, it's about what happens when you're wrong being small, and what happens when you're right being bigger.

Putting it together

A complete trade idea, done properly, answers four questions before you ever open a position:

  • What's the setup? — why this level, why now
  • Where's entry? — the price the idea is built around
  • Where's the stop? — the exact point the idea is wrong
  • Where's the target? — the exact point the idea is right, and what that's worth relative to the risk

If any one of those is missing or vague, what you're looking at isn't really a trade idea — it's a tip. The difference matters more than it sounds.
